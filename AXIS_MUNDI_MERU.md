## 2. Historical & Mathematical Lineage of Sri Meru

### A. Stepped Pyramid Architecture (*Meru Prastara*) & Sacred Geometry
In classical Indian architectural treatises (*Vastu Shastra* and *Shilpa Shastra*), *Meru* refers both to a mythic central mountain and to a specific class of **stepped, multi-tiered temple elevations** (*Meru Prastara*). When a planar *Sri Yantra* (composed of interlocking upward- and downward-pointing triangles bounded by a square enclosure, the *Bhupura*) is extruded vertically into stepped terraces, it forms the three-dimensional **Sri Meru**:

* **Dual-Polarity Interlocking Lattices:** Four upward-pointing triangles (historically classified under *Shiva / Prakasha*, representing structural projection) intersect five downward-pointing triangles (*Shakti / Vimarsha*, representing dynamic modulation) around a central apex point (*Bindu*).
* **Eight-Tier Decomposition & Octonion Algebra:** Excluding the central apex singularity, the classical *Sri Yantra* decomposes into seven concentric geometric circuits (the outer square gate, two lotus-petal rings, and four nested triangular circuits). This `1 + 7` partition maps cleanly onto an **8-dimensional octonion** (`O = x_0 + sum(x_i * e_i)` for `i = 1..7`), where the central apex corresponds to the real scalar axis (`x_0`) and the seven concentric rings correspond to the seven imaginary basis units (`e_1..e_7`) governed by Fano-plane triad multiplication.

### B. Classical Sanskrit Combinatorics & Phonetic Matrices
Classical Indian scholarship developed rigorous formal methods for analyzing sound and combinatorics—most notably **Panini's** *Aṣṭādhyāyī* (generative grammatical rules) and **Pingala's** *Chandaḥśāstra* (combinatorial prosody and binary sequences, including *Meru Prastara*, the stepped triangular array known in European mathematics centuries later as Pascal's triangle).
